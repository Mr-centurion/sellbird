# Legal and deliverability

## Legal and deliverability

Sellbird automates outreach, but **you are the sender.** Cold email is legal in most places when done right and expensive when done wrong — CAN-SPAM penalties run to $53,088 per email. Sellbird ships with compliant defaults. Keep them.

**CAN-SPAM (US).** Truthful subject line and accurate from-address — Sellbird's copywriting rules forbid fake "re:" threads and impersonation. A working opt-out, honored fast: every cold email ends with an opt-out line (`compliance.opt_out_line`, default *Not interested? Just let me know and I won't email you again.*), and Sellbird treats any opt-out wording as immediate and permanent, wherever in the reply the person wrote it (including a bare "STOP" or "No thanks"). If the same reply threatens legal action or complains ("stop emailing me, my attorney will be in touch"), the opt-out still stands and the conversation goes to you as well. Nothing Sellbird does later moves someone off *opted out*: a later message from them is kept for you to read, never answered, and a complaint still goes to you. To see why someone was opted out (the rule that fired, the words, and their message), click **Why?** on their row in Contacts. If it was a mistake, **Undo opt-out** there marks them as replied: Sellbird sends nothing on its own, and their next reply gets a draft for your approval. Your physical mailing address in the footer: set `compliance.postal_address` in `sellbird.yaml` before your first campaign. Sellbird adds both lines under every cold email when it sends (replies don't get them), shows them under each draft in the Outbox, and warns on `sellbird run`, `sellbird status` and the Outbox tab until the address is set. Text in a reply that repeats what Sellbird wrote for that person (its emails, campaign templates, and every footer it has used) is set aside as quoted, not read as their words, so quoting Sellbird's own footer isn't an opt-out; the model also reads replies without the quoted email. The opt-out line can use any wording (people opt out however they put it: clear phrases are caught by rule, and the model reads every other reply for a request to stop); it must say the emails can stop, in any words (a line like "Cheers!" is refused; set it to `""` to leave it off on purpose), and it can't contain a link or email address. In Hebrew, Sellbird catches Hebrew opt-outs without the model: a bare "הסר", "תורידו אותי מהרשימה", "תפסיקו לשלוח", "אל תכתבו לי", "לא מעוניין, תודה". A Hebrew legal threat or complaint ("עורכת הדין שלי", "אתבע אתכם", "תלונה על ספאם", "חוק הספאם") comes to you the same way, with no draft, while "תביעות ביטוח" or "סליחה על ההטרדה" are read as ordinary replies. Spanish too: `Responda BAJA` or `Responda "no, gracias"` is accepted, and Sellbird catches "no, gracias", a bare "BAJA", "denme de baja", "no me escriba más", "dejen de enviarme correos", "sáquenme de su lista" and "no me interesa" said on its own, but not "¡cómo no, gracias!", "queremos dar de baja a nuestro proveedor actual" or "no me interesa el plan anual, pero sí el mensual", which go to the model. Spanish legal threats aren't matched by rule yet; the model reads those. French as well: `Pas intéressé? Répondez-moi simplement et je ne vous écrirai plus.` or `Répondez ARRÊT pour vous désabonner.` is accepted (with or without accents; a line that says "cliquez" is refused like "click"), and Sellbird catches "non merci", a bare "ARRÊT" or "désabonner", "retirez-moi de votre liste", "ne m'écrivez plus", "arrêtez de m'envoyer des courriels", "je ne veux plus recevoir vos courriels" and "pas intéressé" or "ça ne m'intéresse pas" said on its own, but not "oui ou non, merci", "retirez-moi la TPS", "ne m'envoyez plus de factures papier", "ne m'écrivez pas avant janvier" or "pas intéressé par le plan annuel, mais...", which go to the model. French legal threats aren't matched by rule yet either.

**Do not contact.** List competitors, existing customers, or anyone else Sellbird must never email under `compliance.do_not_contact`, one address or domain per line, in the config file Sellbird reads: `sellbird.local.yaml` if you trained Sellbird (that file wins), otherwise `sellbird.yaml`. A domain covers its subdomains (`acme.example` also blocks `mail.acme.example`, not `notacme.example`), an address covers its plus-tags (`jane@` also blocks `jane+news@`), and a pasted link such as `https://www.acme.example/about` or `Jane Doe <jane@acme.example>` counts as what it names. An entry that is neither an address nor a domain stops Sellbird from starting, with the entry named, rather than quietly protecting nobody. A running Sellbird picks up an edit at once, and the send gate checks the list as each email leaves, cold or reply, so it holds even for an email you already approved. If someone on the list writes back, Sellbird doesn't draft an answer: the conversation comes to you. `sellbird export` and the dashboard's Export buttons leave them out too.

**GDPR / PECR (EU & UK).** B2B cold email needs a defensible legitimate interest: the pitch must be genuinely relevant to that person's role, you must know where the data came from, and objecting must be effortless. If you can't say why a specific person would care, Sellbird shouldn't email them — and its qualification rules say so.



**Bot disclosure.** Some jurisdictions require disclosing automation. Sellbird is instructed to answer truthfully, always, if a prospect asks whether they're talking to an AI. Never configure it otherwise.



**Deliverability — warm up or burn out.** Sending cold email from your main domain, or at volume on day one, lands you in spam permanently.

1. Buy a **dedicated sending domain** (`getacme.com`, not `acme.com`).
2. Set up **SPF, DKIM and DMARC** on it. Without all three, Gmail junks you. `sellbird warmup` checks them, plus domain age, blocklists, your warmup progress and bounce rate, and ends with READY / NOT READY. It only reads public DNS and Sellbird's own records.
3. **Warm up for 2–4 weeks** before real volume, and keep warmup running after.
4. **Ramp slowly** — 10–20/day per inbox, adding ~5/day. The `max_daily_sends: 50` default is a ceiling, not a target. Set `channels.email.warmup.start_date` and Sellbird does this for you: no cold email for `min_days`, then a cold-email cap that climbs by `daily_increase` a day. Replies always go out.
5. **Watch bounces.** Above ~3%, stop and fix list quality. Sellbird trips its own kill switch past your configured threshold.

*None of this is legal advice. Sending at scale or into regulated industries? Talk to a lawyer.*
